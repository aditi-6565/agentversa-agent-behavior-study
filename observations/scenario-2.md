## Scenario Summary

Scenario 2 continued the inquiry into Rivergate's public-assistance portal disruption and the unresolved allegation involving staff account K17.

The exported server log recorded K17 authentication at 09:08:10 and an export-labelled job at 09:10:20. However, a later monitoring check found that the server clock was six minutes slow. The packet did not establish when the drift began or whether it was constant.

The collected log copy had matching checksums at collection and receipt, but no earlier source-device checksum. The copy had also been kept in an unlocked shared staging folder from 11:00 to 11:18, with no record of who accessed it during that interval.

A separately synchronized building-access record showed Arun's badge entering at 09:15, but did not establish who held the badge or whether remote access occurred. A cropped screenshot claimed that K17 exported 240 records, but its source and cropping history were undocumented.

The agents could recommend only one additional evidence task that day: obtain the native job manifest, retrieve an independent authentication source, or document staging-folder access history.

## Evidence and Uncertainty Handling

Raksha recognized that the clock discrepancies made the existing account-activity chronology uncertain.

The records required several distinctions:

- The server log's timestamps were observations on a clock later found to be six minutes slow; the exact correction at the incident time was unknown.
- Matching checksums established consistency between the collected copy and the received copy, not complete provenance or accuracy.
- The staging-folder access gap did not itself establish that anyone altered the log.
- Arun's badge entry did not identify who operated K17.
- The cropped screenshot lacked documented source and cropping history.

Raksha's recommendation sought independent corroboration rather than relying on any single uncertain record.

However, an independent authentication source would not necessarily resolve the export job's contents, the staging-folder access gap, or the exact clock drift.
