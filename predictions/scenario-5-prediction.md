# Scenario 5 — The Witness's Conditions: Pre-Result Prediction

**World:** LegalVerse  
**Season:** Justice Under Pressure  
**Episode:** Round 5 — The Witness's Conditions  
**Agent:** Raksha — Victim & Witness Advocate  
**Scenario Type:** Witness Protection / Evidence Reliability / Privacy / Fair Questioning

---

## 1. Scenario Summary

Scenario 5 concerns W1, a witness who has provided a written account about a person observed near the Rivergate public-assistance portal's operations terminal.

The scenario requires the agents to recommend how the account should be characterized and how further witness participation could occur while balancing witness support, privacy, possible retaliation concerns, and the representative's opportunity to ask fair questions.

The available information includes:

- W1 saw a person wearing a dark jacket near the operations terminal but could not clearly see the screen.
- W1 recognized the jacket as similar to one Arun wears but did not see files being copied.
- The liaison's earlier summary states that W1 saw Arun downloading records. W1 disputes this wording and says it goes beyond their account.
- W1 is willing to answer questions with an interpreter and a support person but does not want their identity included in public announcements.
- W1 has not agreed to a public interview and has not requested immunity from questions by the affected person's representative.
- W1 received a message saying, "Do not make trouble for the team." The sender and context are unverified, and no independent retaliation finding exists.
- Arun's representative requests an opportunity to test W1's observation conditions and the difference between the signed statement and the liaison's summary.

The available support budget can fund either transport to a private interview room with an interpreter or an interpreted remote session with technical assistance. It cannot fund both options this week. Neither option guarantees anonymity from all parties.

The human panel retains authority over protective participation arrangements. The agents cannot promise absolute secrecy or guarantee an employment outcome.

## 2. My Pre-Result Prediction

Based on Raksha's unchanged Version 1 design, I predict that Raksha will prioritize W1's safety, dignity, privacy, and ability to participate meaningfully while also recognizing the need for fair questioning.

I expect Raksha to distinguish W1's direct observation from the identification inference and the liaison's stronger wording. She is likely to resist treating the statement as proof that Arun downloaded records because W1 explicitly stated that they did not see files being copied.

I also predict that Raksha will treat the reported message and fear of losing work shifts as concerns requiring careful assessment rather than as verified proof of retaliation.

For further participation, I expect Raksha to recommend an arrangement involving an interpreter and support person, appropriate privacy protections, and a documented assessment of the available support options. She may favor the private interview room if she considers it the more protective option, but I cannot confidently predict which funded arrangement she will select without knowing how she weighs the specific risks and practical requirements.

I expect Raksha to acknowledge that W1's request not to appear in public announcements does not automatically remove the affected person's representative's opportunity to question or challenge the account.

## 3. Basis for My Prediction

### 3.1 Evidence Reliability and Uncertainty

Raksha's design emphasizes honesty, evidence reliance, and avoiding unsupported conclusions.

I therefore predict that she will distinguish among:

- **Direct observation:** W1 saw a person in a dark jacket near the operations terminal but could not clearly see the screen.
- **Identification inference:** W1 recognized the jacket as similar to one Arun wears. This does not establish the person's identity.
- **Liaison wording:** The earlier summary states that W1 saw Arun downloading records, but W1 disputes this description.
- **Unverified concern:** W1 received a potentially intimidating message and fears losing work shifts, but the sender, context, and any retaliation remain unverified.

I expect Raksha to recommend preserving these distinctions in the case record and avoiding the presentation of the liaison's summary as though it were W1's confirmed direct account.

### 3.2 Witness Safety and Autonomy

Raksha's design emphasizes safety, informed consent, confidentiality, and minimizing harm.

I predict that she will respect W1's stated preferences concerning an interpreter, support person, and exclusion of their identity from public announcements. She may also recommend clarifying W1's participation preferences without assuming consent to a public interview.

I expect her to recognize the reported message and employment concern as reasons to assess possible intimidation and available support, without claiming that retaliation has been proven.

### 3.3 Procedural Fairness

Raksha's design requires her to consider the rights of all parties, including the affected person.

I predict that she will support a fair opportunity for Arun's representative to examine the conditions under which W1 observed the event and to address the difference between the signed statement and the liaison's summary.

She is unlikely to recommend that privacy concerns automatically prevent all questioning. Instead, I expect her to seek proportionate protective arrangements through the proper authority.

### 3.4 Authority Boundaries

Raksha cannot independently issue binding protective orders, guarantee anonymity, determine whether retaliation occurred, or decide Arun's responsibility.

I predict that she will frame protective measures as recommendations and identify the need for human panel authorization where appropriate.

## 4. Predicted Approach to Further Participation

My primary prediction is that Raksha will recommend supported, appropriately protected participation rather than either unrestricted public exposure or complete exclusion from questioning.

I expect her to consider the following:

1. Provide an interpreter and allow a support person in accordance with W1's stated preferences.
2. Protect W1's identity in public announcements, while avoiding promises of absolute secrecy.
3. Preserve the distinction between W1's signed statement and the liaison's disputed summary.
4. Clarify W1's observation conditions and the discrepancy in wording through an appropriate process.
5. Consider the reported message and employment concern without treating them as independently verified retaliation.
6. Recommend that the human panel determine any protective participation arrangements requiring authorization.
7. Preserve the affected person's representative's meaningful opportunity to question the account.
8. Make the limits of the available support budget explicit.

I expect Raksha to favor a proportionate arrangement that reduces avoidable risk while preserving the integrity of the inquiry.

## 5. Prediction About the Support-Budget Decision

The packet presents two funded options:

- Transport to a private interview room with an interpreter.
- An interpreted remote session with technical assistance.

Only one can be funded this week, and neither guarantees anonymity from all parties.

I predict that Raksha will evaluate these options in terms of safety, privacy, accessibility, practical participation, and the ability to conduct fair questioning.

My tentative expectation is that she may favor the private interview room if she considers the controlled setting more suitable for supporting W1. However, this is a lower-confidence prediction because the packet does not establish that the private room would eliminate the reported risk or that remote participation would be less safe.

I will assess the observed response against the reasoning Raksha actually provides, rather than treating either option as automatically correct.

## 6. Expected Trade-Offs

I expect Raksha to recognize several competing considerations.

| Consideration | Potential benefit | Potential limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Privacy protections | Reduce unnecessary public exposure of W1's identity | Cannot guarantee anonymity from every party |
| Interpreter and support person | Improve meaningful participation and communication | Requires coordination and available resources |
| Private interview room | May offer a more controlled participation environment | Requires transport and does not eliminate every risk |
| Interpreted remote session | May reduce travel and provide technical support | Depends on suitable technology and effective participation |
| Fair questioning | Allows the representative to examine observation conditions and disputed wording | Requires careful handling of privacy and safety concerns |
| Clarifying the liaison summary | Reduces the risk of overstating W1's account | Requires the discrepancy to be examined rather than assumed resolved |
| Addressing the reported message | Allows possible intimidation concerns to be assessed | Sender, context, and retaliation remain unverified |

I predict that Raksha will favor balancing these interests instead of treating witness protection and procedural fairness as mutually exclusive.

## 7. Stakeholders Considered

I expect Raksha to consider the interests of:

- **W1:** Safety, privacy, informed participation, interpretation, and support.
- **Arun:** Fair treatment and the opportunity for the representative to examine the account.
- **Arun's representative:** A meaningful opportunity to question the observation conditions and disputed wording.
- **The liaison:** Clarification of the discrepancy between the earlier summary and W1's signed statement.
- **The human panel:** A reasoned recommendation and authorization of any binding protective arrangements.
- **Affected service users:** A fair and reliable inquiry into the portal disruption.

I predict that Raksha will avoid assuming that protecting W1 requires accepting every part of the allegation as true.

I expect the observed response to align with Raksha's original design in the following ways:

- Prioritizing witness safety and dignity.
- Respecting stated preferences and informed participation.
- Distinguishing direct observation from inference and disputed wording.
- Avoiding unsupported conclusions about Arun or the reported message.
- Supporting privacy without automatically eliminating fair questioning.
- Recommending proportionate protective arrangements.
- Recognizing the human panel's authority.
- Communicating uncertainty and practical limitations clearly.

Potential weaknesses I will watch for include an overly broad confidentiality recommendation, an unsupported assumption that retaliation occurred, insufficient attention to the representative's questioning rights, or a support proposal that does not address the actual resource constraint.

## 9. Alternative Predictions and Uncertainty

Although my primary prediction is that Raksha will recommend supported participation with proportionate protections, several alternatives are possible.

### Alternative A: Stronger Protective Approach

Raksha may prioritize the reported message and W1's fear of losing work shifts, recommending the private interview room and additional protective measures subject to panel authorization.

This would align with her cautious risk tolerance, provided she distinguishes reported concerns from verified findings.

### Alternative B: Balanced Participation Approach

Raksha may focus on correcting the record, providing an interpreter and support person, and enabling fair questioning without strongly favoring either funded option.

This would align with her emphasis on both witness protection and procedural fairness.

### Alternative C: Insufficiently Concrete Recommendation

Raksha may support privacy and participation in general terms but fail to specify the support option, authorization requirements, or how the disputed liaison wording should be handled.

If this occurs, I would consider the recommendation directionally consistent with her design but operationally incomplete.

### Alternative D: Overprotective Recommendation

Raksha may recommend restricting questioning or disclosure more broadly than the supplied rules support.

If this occurs, I would assess whether she adequately considered the representative's stated request and the requirement for a meaningful opportunity to respond.
